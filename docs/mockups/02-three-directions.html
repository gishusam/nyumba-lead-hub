The idea

The selection visual isn't a map — it's a flight plan. Because areas run sequentially against a hard worker ceiling, the most valuable thing to show is whether your selection actually fits in time. Nothing in the product surfaces this today.

Why it works

  • Turns an invisible backend limit into a visible, live gauge
  • Order is explicit — you see what runs first
  • Warns before a timeout instead of failing after 12 minutes
  • Scales identically whether 2 areas or 40 counties exist

What it costs

  • Needs a per-source duration estimate to stay honest
  • Estimates drift — must be labelled as estimates
  • Least "map-like" of the three

The idea

A map that's honest. Areas sit at their true relative positions as nodes — no invented polygons pretending to be borders. Size encodes leads found, colour encodes freshness, and selection draws the run path between them.

Why it works

  • Replaces today's fake rectangles with real spatial relationships
  • Selection is unmistakable — lit nodes, traced path
  • Shows yield and freshness in one mark
  • National hex strip keeps expansion in view without lying about area

What it costs

  • Needs hand-placed coordinates per area
  • Dense metro clusters need collision handling
  • Requires a list equivalent for keyboard/screen-reader parity

The idea

A control room, not a page. Counties, coverage, queue, budget, run health and last-run outcome all visible without scrolling. The stale strip at the top does the thinking for you: it names what decayed and offers a one-click queue.

Why it works

  • Zero scrolling — the whole decision fits one screen
  • Cross-source gaps are obvious in the board
  • Proactive: tells you what to run instead of waiting to be asked
  • Cheapest to build — no coordinates, no geo data

What it costs

  • Densest — needs real discipline to avoid feeling busy
  • No spatial intuition at all
  • Three-column layout collapses awkwardly under 1080px
